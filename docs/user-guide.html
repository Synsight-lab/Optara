What is an option?

A standardized contract. You pay a premium today for a right to a payout at expiry — if the market moves your way. If it does not, the contract expires worthless and you lose only the premium. Think of a call on AAPL: you gain if the stock rises, without buying 100 shares.

Call — profit when the market rises

Long upside exposure

Pays when settlement price > strike. Like owning the stock, but with limited premium at risk.

WMON $10 Call, expiry Friday 08:00 UTC
Settles at $12 → pays 0.166 WMON (12-10)/12 × 1
Settles at $9 → pays $0
→ You control 1 WMON notional for $0.40 premium instead of buying 1 WMON for $10.
Put — profit when the market falls

Long downside exposure

Pays when settlement price < strike. Like a short position or protective insurance, in USDC.

WMON $10 Put, expiry Friday
Settles at $8 → pays $2.00 USDC 10-8
Settles at $12 → pays $0
→ Gain downside exposure without shorting spot.

How you earn — with stock-like examples

Two roles, opposite market views. Both trade the same contracts.

Writer — sell exposure, collect premium

Similar to a covered call on stocks

You hold or lock the underlying and sell upside (Call) or downside (Put) exposure. Premium is income.

Lock5 WMON
Sell 5 $10 Calls+$2.00 premium
S ≤ $10 → Keep all: 5 WMON + $2.00. Premium is pure profit.
S = $12 → Pay 0.833 WMON: Keep 4.167 WMON + $2.00. Upside owed above $10.

Max loss capped: Call = 1 WMON − premium; Put = Strike − premium. No liquidation beyond locked collateral.

Buyer — buy exposure, pay premium

Similar to buying a call instead of the stock

You pay premium for leveraged upside (Call) or downside (Put) without buying/holding the full notional.

Pay for 5 Calls$2.00
Control5 WMON ($50 notional)
S = $12 → 0.166 WMON/contract: 0.831 WMON net ≈ +$7.97 on $2.00 (4×). At $15 ≈ +$22.9.
S ≤ $10 → $0: Lose only premium: −$2.00.

Capital efficiency: 5 Calls = $2.00 controls $50 notional. You can also sell early on Kuru if the market moves before Friday.

Risks

Illiquidity: You may not find a counterparty before expiry. Hold until settlement — redemption still works.
Settlement delay: Waits for Chainlink’s first update after expiry (≤ ~1h). Price already fixed at expiry.
Premium ≠ fair value: Kuru quote is what counterparties will pay — can be thin. Use the app’s safety range.

FAQ

Which earns more — writing or buying? ›
Writing = frequent premium, like covered calls. Buying = less frequent but larger upside. Many do both.
Can I lose more than I put in? ›
Buyer: No — max = premium. Writer: Max = collateral − premium (no liquidation beyond).
Must I wait until expiry? ›
No — sell on Kuru any time before expiry. After expiry, wait for settlement then redeem/claim.
Can I close half?
Yes — ERC-20s. Any positive amount, no minimum after minting.
Where are my assets?
In the vault contract — non-upgradeable, not pausable for payouts.
What if Chainlink is unavailable?
If Chainlink never publishes after expiry, the series cannot settle in V1 — disclosed. Use only major direct feeds, ≤30-day expiries.
